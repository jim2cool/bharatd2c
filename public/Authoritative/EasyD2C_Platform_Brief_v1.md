**The Three Commerce Architectures**

**Product Engine**

High-intensity, PDP-led conversion. Built for brands with a hero
product. The page is organised around a Gallery Zone, a Conversion Zone
(Title, Reviews, Offer Cluster, CTA, Trust), a Description Zone, a
Social Proof Zone, and a Cross-Sell Zone. Sticky CTA enforced on mobile.
Above-the-fold composition locked. This is the architecture where
conversion science is most dense.

**Story-First**

Brand narrative-led commerce. For sellers who sell through identity and
emotion. Story sections, ritual guides, and emotional content blocks sit
alongside the standard conversion structure. The brand\'s \"why\" is as
visible as the product\'s price.

**Catalog-First**

Multi-product browsing architecture. For stores with large inventory
across multiple categories. Mega menu, advanced filters, and grid-first
logic enforced. Navigation is the primary conversion instrument.

**The Alive Engine: Motion**

Motion is the last sensory layer. Every other layer is visible in a
screenshot. Motion only exists in real time, in a browser, when a real
buyer is scrolling. It is what makes a store feel alive or dead. The Alive Engine is a set of seven motion primitives, calibrated per
mood card, that run across the entire storefront without compromising
load time or accessibility.

- FadeIn --- staggered section and component entry as the buyer scrolls.
  Fires once. Never re-animates.

- SlideIn --- directional slide entrance for panels, drawers, and
  side-entering elements. Direction is configurable: bottom, top,
  left, right.

- ScaleTap --- tactile scale feedback on every CTA, button, and product
  card. The single change that makes the entire store feel premium on
  mobile.

- StaggerGroup --- container that sequences entrance animations across
  a list of children. Used for benefit lists, testimonial rows, and
  component grids.

- Magnetic --- cursor-aware primary CTA on desktop. The CTA follows the
  pointer subtly, making it feel alive. Calibrated per mood card from
  barely perceptible (Shaahi) to highly responsive (Dhamaka).

- Parallax --- depth on hero images and gallery photography as the buyer
  scrolls. Desktop only. Calibrated per mood card --- most pronounced on
  Shaahi and Rasoi where photography is the primary emotional
  instrument.

- Shimmer --- loading skeletons and, on Dhamaka and Taza only, a single
  light sweep across the CTA on page load that draws the eye to the
  action immediately.

All motion respects the prefers-reduced-motion accessibility setting.
When active, duration drops to zero and scale drops to one --- all
motion disappears instantly with no code changes anywhere.

**5. The RTO Intelligence Engine**

> ***The RTO Intelligence Engine is not a risk tool. It is a margin
> protection system with a network effect. Every order makes it smarter.
> Every store that joins makes it smarter for every store already on the
> platform.***

Return to Origin is the defining margin problem for Indian D2C sellers.
COD-driven RTO costs the industry an estimated ₹15,000+ crore annually.
Every seller who has built their own mitigation has done so by
sacrificing reach to reduce risk --- blanket pincode blocks, static
blacklists, OTP walls. This is the wrong trade-off.

Easy D2C\'s position is different: minimise RTO without minimising
reach. Not by blocking bad orders --- by converting risky orders into
safe ones, and building the intelligence to know the difference at the
moment of checkout.

**How Scoring Works**

Every COD order receives a composite risk score from 0 to 100. The score
is computed from four signal layers:

- Base signals --- historical blacklist pincodes, IndiaPost API pincode
  verification, customer bounce history, logistics-difficult states,
  fake phone number patterns, incomplete address strings.

- Extended signals --- order time, order value bands, address velocity,
  cross-store phone matching, device fingerprint patterns, VPN and proxy
  detection.

- Session behaviour signals --- the Adaptive Engine\'s data fed directly
  into risk scoring. Returning visitors score lower risk. Checkout
  completed in under 45 seconds scores higher risk. Review section dwell
  reduces risk. Rage clicks increase it.

- Category risk multipliers --- Dropshipping at 1.4x, Fashion at 1.3x,
  Electronics at 1.2x, down to Food at 0.8x. Continuously updated from
  platform delivery outcomes.

The cross-store signal is structurally unique to Easy D2C. A phone
number that bounced a delivery on another store on the platform is
flagged on every store. No independent seller, and no other platform,
can see this signal.

**The Living Pincode Intelligence Database**

The pincode risk database is not a static download. It is continuously
self-updating from every delivery outcome across every store on the
platform. At launch it is seeded from IndiaPost API data, public
logistics-difficult region data, and industry benchmarks. From day one
of live orders, every delivery outcome updates the database --- by
pincode, category, order value band, and time of order. Pincodes that
spike in RTO rate are automatically elevated platform-wide. Pincodes
with 90 consecutive days of clean delivery are automatically downgraded.

This is the network effect moat. A seller with 50 orders a month cannot
build this intelligence alone. A platform with thousands of stores and
hundreds of thousands of orders a month can. Every store\'s delivery
data makes every other store\'s protection stronger.

**6. Payments Infrastructure**

Getting a payment gateway live is not a feature request for Easy D2C
sellers --- it is the prerequisite that unlocks the RTO Engine\'s most
powerful interventions. Solving gateway onboarding is therefore not a
payments decision. It is an RTO strategy decision.

**The Problem**

Setting up a payment gateway in India as a small or first-time seller is
genuinely painful. Razorpay, Cashfree, and PayU each require business
registration documents, a GST certificate, a cancelled cheque, and a
website review that typically takes 5--10 business days. The result: a
large proportion of small D2C sellers operate COD-only --- not because
they prefer it, but because they never completed gateway setup. These
are exactly the sellers who need the RTO Engine most.

**What Easy D2C Cannot Do --- The Regulatory Reality**

The most intuitive solution --- Easy D2C collecting buyer payments
directly and reimbursing sellers --- is not a product feature. It is a
regulated financial service. In India, collecting money on behalf of
third-party merchants makes an entity a Payment Aggregator under the
RBI\'s PA/PG framework. Operating as a PA without a license carries
penalties including forced shutdown and personal liability for
directors. A full PA license requires minimum net worth of ₹25 crore,
full AML and KYC compliance, and a 12--18 month application process
minimum. This is a Phase 4 consideration, not a Year 1 shortcut.
